What you are seeing

    Science meets imagination

    Uranus rolls around the Sun on its side — thin, charcoal-dark rings standing nearly vertical to its orbit. Only Voyager 2 has visited, in 1986. The band layout, moonlet, and tilt marker are artistic reconstructions, not survey data.

    The flight is far gentler than a real ring-plane crossing. Press G to compare Earth gravity — Uranus pulls almost identically. Listen for rare grains ticking off the hull inside the bands.

    Click the small Sun or the giant itself in the sky to learn about it — you can then visit it in the Solar System view.

    Voyager 2 is a real NASA 3D model — the only probe ever to visit Uranus. The planet uses a real texture; the ring sheet, particle field, and starfield are generated locally.

    IN URANUS'S SKY

    See it in the Solar System

    About this expedition

    What is it like to fly through the rings of Uranus?

    Drift through the narrow, charcoal-dark rings of Uranus in an imagined probe, with a real NASA 3D model of Voyager 2 flying beside you. The turquoise planet fills half the sky, and the rings curve around it as thin concentric bands — nothing like Saturn’s broad, bright sheets.

    Uranus is an ice giant that rolls around the Sun on its side: its axis is tilted about 98°, so each pole gets roughly 42 years of continuous daylight followed by 42 years of night. Its 13 known rings are narrow and darker than coal, reflecting only a few percent of the sunlight that hits them. Methane in its atmosphere absorbs red light and gives the planet its blue-green colour. Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft ever to visit, flying past in January 1986.

    Rings

    Five discoveries to find

    1. The ring vista — the thin rings, with the ε ring the brightest of them.
    2. A ring charcoal lump — a nearly black ring particle in the specimen viewer.
    3. The dark gap — the quiet space between narrow rings.
    4. The shepherd moonlet — a small moon that keeps a ring edge sharp.
    5. The 98° tilt — a marker globe that shows how far Uranus leans.

    How to explore

    Press Begin the flight. The probe cruises forward on its own; W A S D drift sideways, Space rises and C sinks below the ring plane. Drag to look around; on phones and tablets a touch pad appears. Press G to compare the local gravity with Earth’s, P for photo mode, and E to record a discovery in your journal — progress is saved on your own device.

    Science and artistic reconstruction

    The ring count, darkness and the planet’s tilt come from Voyager 2 and telescope observations; Voyager 2 is a real NASA 3D model. The band layout, moonlet and tilt marker are artistic reconstructions, and the scene is compressed for viewing.
