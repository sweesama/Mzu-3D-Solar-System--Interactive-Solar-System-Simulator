Explore the Solar System in 3D: drag to orbit the camera, scroll or pinch to zoom, and select a world to inspect it. Start the short guided tour if you are not sure where to begin.

What you can do in this solar system simulator

  • Fly around the Sun and all eight planets, with major moons such as the Moon, Phobos and Deimos, the Galilean moons of Jupiter and Saturn's rings. Click any object for its radius, distance from the Sun and orbital eccentricity.
  • Switch between artistic and real scale. The default view compresses sizes and distances so everything fits on screen. Real distances, Real sizes and True scale show how empty and how large the Solar System really is.
  • Run time at real speed. Set the clock from one hour to one year per second and watch the planets move on their true orbital and rotation periods.
  • Take the guided planet tour for a short introduction to each world, or open a planet's own page for facts and frequently asked questions.
  • Leave orbit and explore in first person: walk on the Moon, Mars, Venus and Mercury, descend into Jupiter, or fly through the rings and storms of the outer planets.
  • Share, save and embed the exact view you are looking at as a link, an image or an iframe.

How to use the 3D solar system

Drag to rotate the view, scroll or pinch to zoom, and click or tap a planet to focus on it. Press G to hide this guide and see the whole scene. The Speed, Scale and Quality menus are in the corner of the screen.

Frequently asked questions

Is this 3D Solar System to scale?

Not by default. Real distances are so large that the planets would be invisible dots, so sizes and distances are compressed for viewing. Open the Scale menu and choose Real distances, Real sizes or True scale to see the real proportions, and use the Speed menu to switch motion to real orbital and rotation periods.

Does it show where the planets are today?

No. The planets move on their orbits, but their starting positions are not taken from a live ephemeris, so this is not a tool for finding a planet in tonight's sky. Use it to compare sizes, distances and orbital periods, and to explore the worlds up close.

Is it free, and do I need to install anything?

It is free and runs in any modern browser with WebGL, on desktop or mobile. There is no download, account or plug-in.

Can I explore the surface of other planets?

Yes. The first-person expeditions let you walk on the Moon, Mars, Venus and Mercury, sink into Jupiter's clouds, and fly through the rings of Saturn and Uranus and the storms of Neptune.

Does it work on phones and tablets?

Yes. Drag with one finger to orbit, pinch to zoom and tap a planet to select it. On older devices, set Quality to Low or leave it on Auto, which lowers the detail if the frame rate drops.

Can I use it in a classroom or embed it on my website?

Yes. Use Share view to copy a link that opens on the planet you are looking at, or Copy embed to get an iframe snippet for a class page or blog. Links such as /?focus=Saturn open straight on a planet.
