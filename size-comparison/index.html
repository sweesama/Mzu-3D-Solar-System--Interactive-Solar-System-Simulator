Frequently asked questions

Which planet is the biggest?

Jupiter. Its equatorial diameter is 142,984 km, 11.21 times Earth’s, using the NASA Planetary Fact Sheet figures on this site.

Which planet is the smallest?

Mercury, at 4,879 km across, 0.38 times Earth’s.

How much bigger is Jupiter than Earth?

Jupiter is 11.21 times wider than Earth. The circles use equatorial diameter. A volume comparison is the diameter ratio cubed, and this page does not show volume.

How is this different from the sizes and distances page?

The sizes and distances page is the reference: diameters, Sun distances, years, a 1-metre Sun scale model, and the wider fact-sheet table. This page is the visual which-is-bigger comparison. Neither replaces the other.

Are the circles to scale?

Yes, against each other, by equatorial diameter. Saturn’s rings are not included; the circle is the globe. The Sun is 1,391,400 km across, about 109 times Earth’s diameter, and is left off so the planets stay visible. The sizes and distances page shows that larger scale.

Why do Earth and Venus look almost the same?

Venus is 12,104 km across and Earth is 12,756 km. Venus is about 95% of Earth’s diameter.
