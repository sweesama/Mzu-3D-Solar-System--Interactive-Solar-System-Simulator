What you are seeing

    Science meets imagination

    Saturn's rings are countless ice particles orbiting in a sheet often only tens of metres thick yet about 270,000 km across — roughly 70% of the way from Earth to the Moon. Cassini studied them from orbit for thirteen years, then dived through the gap between rings and planet in 2017. The band layout, propeller swirl, and moonlet are artistic reconstructions, not survey data.

    The cruise is far gentler than real ring-plane flight. Press G to feel the ring's faint tidal tug ease under Earth's gravity. Listen for grains ticking off the hull in the dense bands.

    Click the small Sun or the giant itself in the sky to learn about it — you can then visit it in the Solar System view.

    Cassini is a real NASA 3D model. The Saturn globe uses a real planet texture; the ring sheet, ice field, and starfield are generated locally.

    About this expedition

    What is it like to fly through Saturn’s rings?

    Cruise the ring plane of Saturn in an imagined probe, with NASA’s Cassini spacecraft — a real NASA 3D model — flying alongside. Ice boulders tumble past, the bright B ring and the dark Cassini Division curve around the golden planet, and a small shepherd moon herds the ring edge.

    Saturn’s main rings are made of countless pieces of water ice, from dust-sized grains to boulders several metres across. Together they span about 270,000 km — roughly 70% of the distance from Earth to the Moon — yet they are often only tens of metres thick. The widest gap, the Cassini Division, is about 4,800 km across. Cassini orbited Saturn from 2004 to 2017 and ended its mission with 22 dives between the rings and the planet.

    Five discoveries to find

    1. The ring approach — the B ring, C ring and Cassini Division from above.
    2. A chunk of ring ice — a tumbling ice boulder in the specimen viewer.
    3. The great division — inside the Cassini Division, a gap that is not quite empty.
    4. The shepherd moonlet — a small moon that keeps ring edges sharp.
    5. The propeller — the S-shaped wake of a moonlet too small to clear a gap.

    How to explore

    Press Begin the cruise. The probe cruises forward on its own; W A S D drift sideways, Space rises and C sinks below the ring sheet. Drag to look around; on phones and tablets a touch pad appears. Press G to compare the local gravity with Earth’s, P for photo mode, and E to record a discovery in your journal — progress is saved on your own device.

    Science and artistic reconstruction

    Ring composition, structure and the Cassini model are based on NASA science and 3D Resources, and the Saturn globe uses a real planet texture. The band layout, the propeller and the moonlet are artistic reconstructions, not survey data.

    穿越土星环是什么感觉？

    以一艘虚构探测器的身份在土星环平面上巡航，NASA 的卡西尼号（真实三维模型）并肩飞行。冰块翻滚着掠过，明亮的 B 环和暗色的卡西尼环缝绕着这颗金色行星弯曲，一颗小小的牧羊卫星正在约束环的边缘。

    土星的主环由无数水冰颗粒组成，从尘粒到数米宽的巨砾不等。整个环系宽约 27 万公里（约为地月距离的七成），但厚度往往只有几十米。最宽的缝隙卡西尼环缝约 4,800 公里宽。卡西尼号 2004 至 2017 年环绕土星，任务末期进行了多次穿越环与行星之间缝隙的俯冲。

    五个发现

    1. 环面远眺 — 从高处看 B 环、C 环和卡西尼环缝。
    2. 环冰的一块 — 标本查看器中翻滚的冰块。
    3. 巨大的缝隙 — 卡西尼环缝内，并非真正空无一物。
    4. 牧羊小卫星 — 让环边缘保持锐利的小卫星。
    5. 螺旋桨 — 太小而无法清出完整缝隙的小卫星留下的 S 形涟漪。
